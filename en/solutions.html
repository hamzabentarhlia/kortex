The shift

Why does traditional content stop working?

Because buyers increasingly ask answer engines instead of scrolling result pages, and those engines quote sources they can understand and attribute — not the pages with the most keywords. Content built for rankings is often invisible to the systems that now write the answer.

Traditional SEO content

  • —3,000-word "ultimate guides" that answer nothing specifically and satisfy no one precisely.
  • —Chasing keyword density and vanity traffic metrics that do not translate into commercial authority.
  • —Losing the founder's authentic voice to generic ghostwriters working from a blank brief.
  • —Publishing at volume without a coherent entity footprint: visible in bursts, never legible at scale.
  • —Measuring success by traffic rank — a metric answer engines do not use when they choose a source.

KORTEX GEO

  • +Precise, answer-first blocks for ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini, formatted for direct citation.
  • +Dense semantic entity clusters that a model can attribute to one source: your brand.
  • +The logic comes from the founder's own recording — the conviction already exists; the system converts it.
  • +One source cycle producing a coherent entity graph across every surface: pillar, FAQ, LinkedIn, email, sales.
  • +Success measured by citation authority: whether AI models name your company when the question is relevant.

The system

How does the KORTEX GEO pipeline work?

It runs four layers in a fixed order — Signal, Structure, Answers, Distribution — on every engagement. The inputs change from client to client; the architecture does not. Each layer has defined inputs, defined tools and a defined deliverable, so nothing depends on inspiration.

01 Signal

Layer 1 — Source Signal Extraction

You record. We extract. The raw recording is transcribed and mapped for vocabulary, argument structure, recurring examples, contrarian positions and the objections the founder instinctively answers. Nothing is invented: everything already exists in the source, and the system surfaces it.

The voice fingerprint — the specific phrasing that makes the founder recognisable — is locked at this stage and carried into every downstream asset, so the output sounds like you rather than like a generic AI writer.

What happens
Audio is transcribed, cleaned and segmented by topic; claims, examples, numbers you actually said and objections are tagged and timestamped.
Input
One founder video, podcast episode, webinar, sales demo or Loom (45–60 minutes is ideal) plus a short intake form.
Tools
Whisper for transcription, Gemini for structured extraction, n8n to orchestrate the workflow end to end.
Output
A source dossier that every later layer is built from — and that you can reuse internally.
Deliverables
  • Clean transcript
  • Voice fingerprint
  • Argument map
  • FAQ seed list
02 Structure

Layer 2 — Semantic Entity Structuring

AI models do not read the way people do; they map relationships between entities. We translate the extraction into a stable entity structure: the category you own, the problem you solve, the mechanism you use, the alternative you reject and the audience you serve. These definitions are fixed in language and reused identically across every asset.

Consistent terminology is the primary GEO lever. Models attribute authority to sources they can cluster, and fragmented language prevents clustering. This is the layer most content teams skip — see our guide to entity-first content architecture.

What happens
We write one-sentence definitions for your category, problem, mechanism and audience, and choose the exact names that will be repeated everywhere.
Input
The source dossier from Layer 1 and your existing site, offer and positioning.
Tools
Gemini for drafting and consistency checks, n8n to push the canon into every production template.
Output
An entity map and terminology canon that govern every page and post.
Deliverables
  • Category definition
  • Mechanism name
  • Terminology canon
  • Audience spec
03 Answers

Layer 3 — Answer Asset Production

The source is split into a controlled publishing system. Every asset covers a specific stage of the buyer journey and of the retrieval cycle. Pillar articles define the thesis. Contrarian pieces sharpen the position. Comparison pages capture mid-funnel demand. FAQ clusters map the follow-up questions an answer engine generates from the pillar.

Every asset uses answer-first formatting: the core claim appears in the first paragraph, definitions are stated early, and structured blocks give models clean extraction points without having to parse long prose.

What happens
Drafts are generated from the dossier and the canon, then edited for accuracy, voice and answer-first structure; FAQPage and Article schema are prepared for each page.
Input
Entity map, terminology canon, argument map and FAQ seed list.
Tools
Gemini for drafting, n8n for templating, internal linking and schema generation, plus a human editorial pass.
Output
Publish-ready pages with internal links and structured data already in place.
Deliverables
  • Pillar article
  • Contrarian article
  • Comparison page
  • FAQ cluster (10–15 Q&A)
  • Use-case blocks
04 Distribution

Layer 4 — Multi-Surface Distribution

LinkedIn posts, newsletter angles, outreach sequences and offer-page updates are all derived from the same source and all point back to the same entity graph. Distribution is not separate from content: it is the repeated-exposure layer that gives your entity graph surface area on every channel where prospects discover a category.

Every post routes the right reader into the retrieval layer. Every sequence uses the mechanism language the source pages establish. The message compounds instead of fragmenting — the opposite of LinkedIn content that dies in 48 hours.

What happens
Posts and emails are written in hook, mechanism, contrarian, decision and operator-take formats, each linking to a source page, and scheduled in the publishing order below.
Input
The published answer assets from Layer 3 and your voice fingerprint.
Tools
Gemini for variants, n8n for the publishing calendar and hand-off to your scheduling tools.
Output
A ready-to-post distribution kit aligned with the pages it promotes.
Deliverables

The sequence

How does a 30-day cycle run, week by week?

A cycle runs in five steps: a kickoff, then four weeks in which the retrieval layer goes live first and distribution follows. Publishing order is not arbitrary — the pages answer engines should cite must exist before LinkedIn and email start sending people to them. The full logic is explained in the one-video authority system.

  1. 00
    Week 0 — Kickoff

    Audit, intake and source recording

    We review your site and current visibility in answer engines, agree on the category and the questions you want to own, and you send or record the source (under 60 minutes of your time).

    • You provide: the recording and access to your site/CMS (or we deliver files).
    • You receive: an audit summary and the cycle plan.
  2. 01
    Week 1 — Days 1–7

    Signal, structure and the retrieval anchor

    Layers 1 and 2 run: transcript, argument map, entity map and terminology canon. The pillar article goes live first (day 1 of publishing), internally linked to your solution and pricing pages. On days 2–3 the first LinkedIn post — the hook — points to it. The contrarian article follows on day 5, and the FAQ cluster is deployed on day 7.

    • By the end of week 1, pillar, contrarian article and FAQ are live, linked and indexable: a model can traverse the entity graph.
  3. 02
    Week 2 — Days 8–14

    Comparison page and the LinkedIn wave

    Around day 12 the comparison page goes live to capture buyers already weighing options. LinkedIn posts covering the mechanism, the decision framework and contrarian takes begin and continue through day 25.

  4. 03
    Week 3 — Days 15–21

    Distribution at cadence

    The post calendar keeps running, each post routing back to a source page. The educational newsletter goes to the cold audience from day 20, and we prepare the sales assets with the same mechanism language.

  5. 04
    Week 4 — Days 22–30

    Amplification, sales activation and review

    The opinionated newsletter goes to your warm list, outreach sequences are updated, and CTA blocks are deployed across offer pages (days 20–28). The cycle closes with a review of what was published and which questions the next source cycle should target.

The technical layer

Why does this content get cited?

Because every asset is engineered around six traits that make a page easy for an answer engine to extract, understand and attribute. None of them is a trick; together they make your company the clearest source on a question.

BLUF formatting

Bottom Line Up Front. Every page answers its core question in the first paragraph. Models look for an extractable claim; a generic preamble loses the citation before it begins.

Opinion density

Generic observations get synthesised around. Named positions get cited. Every asset contains at least one quotable claim that a model can attribute to your company by name.

FAQPage schema

Structured data on every FAQ cluster tells crawlers the page contains question-and-answer content, making each answer an explicit, machine-readable unit.

Semantic adjacency

Pages cover the conceptual territory around the main thesis, not just the thesis itself, showing that the source understands the full problem landscape.

Internal source reinforcement

Several pages strengthen the same entity from different angles, so a model sees a coherent authority cluster rather than isolated pages.

Terminology canon

The same mechanism name, category label and contrasting alternatives used identically on every page and post. Consistency is what lets a model cluster and attribute with confidence.

Knowledge base

Questions answered.

What is Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)?

GEO is the practice of structuring content so that answer engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews — retrieve and cite your brand as the authoritative source for a category-relevant question. Unlike traditional SEO, which targets search result rankings, GEO targets citation authority: being the source a model names. The key levers are entity precision, answer-first formatting, opinion density, semantic adjacency and internal source reinforcement.

What is the difference between GEO and SEO?

SEO optimises for search result rankings using keywords, backlinks and click-through rates, and measures success in traffic. GEO optimises for AI retrieval and citation using entity clarity, answer-first formatting, consistent terminology and internal source reinforcement, and measures success by whether your company is named as a source in AI-generated answers.

How much of the founder's time does this require?

Under 60 minutes per source cycle. Founders provide the raw signal — a recorded video, podcast, webinar or Loom. No writing, no briefs, no endless review rounds. KORTEX handles extraction, entity structuring, asset production, internal linking and distribution setup. The founder's job is to have the conversation; ours is to convert it.

What deliverables does KORTEX produce per source cycle?

One pillar article, one contrarian article, one comparison page, one FAQ cluster of 10–15 questions, 8–12 LinkedIn posts, 2 newsletter angles, CTA blocks, landing-page proof points, outreach lines and call scripts. All outputs share the same entity structure, terminology and internal linking architecture.

How long before we see results?

We plan for first entity signals in AI-generated answers within 4–8 weeks of deploying a complete answer asset layer (pillar article, FAQ cluster and comparison page). Consistent citation as a named source for category queries builds over 3–6 months of coherent source cycles. These are working timeframes, not guarantees: answer engines change often and no one controls what they cite.

What if we do not have existing video content?

A 45–60 minute recorded conversation structured around your core thesis, mechanism, contrarian position and frequent questions is enough. The recording does not need to be produced for public release: it is a source document, not a publish-ready asset. A Loom or a structured video call with our team is sufficient to launch the first cycle.

Does this work alongside existing SEO efforts?

Yes. The KORTEX system is additive. Technical SEO fundamentals — crawlability, page speed, schema markup, canonical URLs — remain the baseline and are respected in every asset we produce. GEO builds the authority layer on top of that foundation, optimising for citation and retrieval rather than keyword rank alone.

Which tools power the KORTEX pipeline?

n8n orchestrates the workflows, Whisper transcribes the source recording, and the Gemini API handles structured extraction and drafting. Every asset then goes through an editorial pass for accuracy, voice and structure before delivery. You own all the content produced.

Who is this built for?

Founder-led B2B companies where the CEO or operator has a real point of view and existing source material — webinars, podcasts, demos, sales-call recordings — that is not being turned into a retrievable authority system: SaaS teams, expert consultancies and operator-led brands whose insight is trapped in formats with a short shelf life.
