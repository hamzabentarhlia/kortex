Start with the basics

What is GEO, and where should you start?

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of making your content easy for AI answer engines to understand, trust and cite. Traditional SEO competes for a position on a results page. GEO competes for a mention inside the answer itself: when a buyer asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Google AI Overviews a question about your category, either your company is named as a source or it is not.

That changes what “good content” means. Keyword density and publishing frequency matter less. What matters is entity clarity (the model knows exactly who you are and what you claim), answer-first structure (definitions, lists, comparisons and FAQs it can lift cleanly), a distinct point of view, and a tightly linked set of pages that reinforce the same thesis from several angles.

This blog is organised to get you from zero to a working GEO system. If you are new to the topic, follow the three-step reading path. If you already publish regularly, start with the content audit framework for AI retrieval to find out which of your existing pages can be retrofitted.

The audit framework

How do you know if a page is GEO-ready?

Score each page from 0 to 2 on five criteria, for a total out of 10. It takes about five minutes per page and tells you whether to keep, retrofit or rebuild. The full method is in the AI retrieval audit guide.

01 — BLUF extractability (0–2 points)

Does the first paragraph state the core claim?

2: the opening paragraph contains a precise, extractable claim with the entity name or mechanism. 1: the claim appears in the first section but not the opening. 0: the page opens with background or rhetorical questions.

02 — Entity clarity (0–2 points)

Is the terminology consistent and canonical?

2: entity name and mechanism appear in the first 200 words and are repeated consistently. 1: present but inconsistent, or generic phrasing. 0: no entity terminology, generic language only.

03 — Structural answer blocks (0–2 points)

Are there clean extraction points?

2: an FAQ section, comparison table or definition blocks under clear headings. 1: numbered lists or sub-headings but no direct-answer format. 0: unbroken narrative prose only.

04 — Schema markup (0–2 points)

Is the content machine-readable?

2: Article schema plus FAQPage where relevant, with a real description. 1: basic Article schema with an empty or generic description. 0: no structured data beyond CMS defaults.

05 — Internal linking (0–2 points)

Is the entity graph connected?

2: links to the pillar page and at least two related pages using canonical anchor text. 1: internal links present but with generic anchors. 0: no internal links, or an orphaned page.

8–10GEO-ready: maintain
4–7Retrofit: prioritise
0–3Retire or rebuild

The 30-day cycle

How does one video become 30 days of assets?

This is the production sequence KORTEX runs for every source cycle: one founder video in, a linked set of answer assets and distribution posts out. The detailed walkthrough is in from one video to a 30-day authority system.

1
Days 1–2 · Extraction & entity structure

Source analysis and definitions

The founder video (ideally 45–60 minutes) is transcribed. An entity structure document is created: category name, mechanism, alternative, audience and terminology canon. The founder's voice is captured so every asset sounds like them.

2
Days 3–5 · Pillar article

The core authority asset

A pillar article of 2,000–2,500 words defines the category, introduces the mechanism and rejects the alternative. Answer-first opening, 3–4 FAQ blocks, definition sections, Article schema and internal link slots.

3
Days 6–8 · FAQ cluster & schema

The retrieval layer

10–15 questions with FAQPage markup. Each answer starts with the direct claim. Questions are phrased the way buyers actually ask them. Schema is validated.

4
Days 9–11 · Contrarian & comparison

Positioning assets

A contrarian article (1,200–1,500 words) names a false belief and rejects it with mechanism logic. A comparison page (1,000–1,200 words) sets out a labelled head-to-head. Both link to the pillar with canonical anchor text.

5
Days 12–15 · Internal linking

The entity graph is wired

The pillar links to the FAQ, contrarian and comparison pages; each supporting page links back. The solution and contact pages are linked from the pillar. Navigation is tested.

6
Days 16–22 · Distribution assets

The social layer

12–16 LinkedIn posts (hooks, mechanism explainers, contrarian takes), 2 newsletter angles and 3–5 short clips cut from the video. All use the terminology canon and point back to the pillar.

7
Days 23–25 · Publishing & indexing

Content goes live

Pillar, FAQ, contrarian and comparison pages are published and checked for indexing. LinkedIn posts start going out (1–2 per day). The email list is notified.

8
Days 26–30 · Monitoring & optimisation

Retrieval tracking begins

A fixed set of category questions is run in the answer engines to set a baseline. Analytics are reviewed, the terminology canon is updated with real market language, and the next cycle is planned.

Execution frameworks

What is the minimum viable GEO footprint?

Four linked assets are enough to give answer engines an unambiguous picture of one topic. Everything else deepens the same graph.

The minimum viable GEO footprint

Asset 1 — Pillar article (2,000–2,500 words)

Defines the category, mechanism and audience, and rejects the alternative. Answer-first opening, 3–4 definition sections, internal links to every supporting asset. Published first; it becomes the primary retrieval anchor.

Asset 2 — Contrarian article (1,200–1,500 words)

Names and challenges the dominant false belief in the category, positioned against the pillar's mechanism. Links back to the pillar using the mechanism name as anchor text.

Asset 3 — Comparison page (1,000–1,200 words)

A structured head-to-head of your mechanism vs the alternative, with a labelled table. Captures mid-funnel demand and links to the pillar and the FAQ cluster.

Asset 4 — FAQ cluster (10–15 Q&As with FAQPage schema)

Answer-first responses to questions phrased as buyers ask them. Often the single most quotable asset for answer engines because each answer is self-contained.

Time to deploy: 14–20 days

A minimum viable entity graph you can start monitoring. Each following source cycle deepens the same graph instead of fragmenting it.

The terminology canon (document template)

Entity name

The exact, unchanging name used everywhere. Example: “KORTEX”, not “Kortex” or “the Kortex platform”.

Category label

The phrase that names the problem space, never rotated with synonyms. Example: “AI content infrastructure”.

Mechanism name

The named approach, used identically everywhere. Example: “source extraction”, not alternately “repurposing” or “transformation”.

Problem statement

A one-sentence definition of what the entity solves, worded the same way in every article's opening.

Alternative name

The precise phrase for what the entity replaces. Example: “traditional SEO keyword publishing”.

Audience qualifier

Exact phrasing for who it is for. Example: “founder-led B2B companies”.

The internal linking pattern

Pillar article

Links to the FAQ cluster, contrarian article, comparison page, solution page and contact page, using the canonical mechanism name as anchor text.

FAQ cluster

Links back to the pillar from every relevant answer, anchored on the mechanism name or category label.

Contrarian article

Links to the pillar (mechanism definition) and the comparison page (alternative positioning).

Comparison page

Links to the pillar, to the FAQ cluster for follow-up questions, and to contact for evaluation.

Social and email

Every LinkedIn post and newsletter links to the pillar or the most relevant supporting asset.

GEO glossary

The key GEO terms, defined.

Short, precise definitions of the vocabulary used across this blog. Each one is written to stand on its own.

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)
The practice of structuring content so AI answer engines can extract, attribute and cite it. GEO targets source authority (being named in the answer) rather than a ranking position. See GEO vs SEO.
Answer engine
A system that responds to a question with a synthesised answer instead of a list of links, such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Google AI Overviews. It may show the sources it relied on.
Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)
A closely related term for optimising content to be selected and quoted by answer engines. In practice it overlaps almost entirely with GEO; see the answer engine visibility guide.
AI citation
When an answer engine names your company, links your page or attributes a specific claim to you as a source. The core outcome GEO aims for.
Being synthesized around
When a model uses the general idea from your content without naming you, blending it into a generic answer. Typical of content that anyone in the industry could have written.
Entity
A uniquely identifiable thing (a company, product, person, method or category) that a model can recognise and connect to other things. Clear entities are easier to attribute.
Entity-first content
A content architecture where every page reinforces a small set of clearly named entities and their relationships, rather than chasing new topics. See entity-first architecture.
BLUF (bottom line up front)
Writing pattern that states the answer or main claim in the first sentence or paragraph, then elaborates. It gives answer engines a clean, quotable extraction point.
Terminology canon
A reference document fixing the exact words used for your entity, category, mechanism, alternative and audience, so every asset describes you identically.
Pillar article
The long-form page that defines your category and mechanism and links to all supporting assets. It is the main retrieval anchor of an authority system.
Authority system
KORTEX's term for the linked set of assets produced from one founder video: pillar, FAQ cluster, comparison and contrarian pages, and distribution posts that all reinforce the same entity.
Structured data (schema markup)
Machine-readable JSON-LD annotations, such as Article or FAQPage, that describe what a page contains and help engines parse it reliably.

FAQ

Questions about GEO and this blog.

What is the KORTEX blog about?

The KORTEX blog covers Generative Engine Optimization (GEO): how B2B companies can structure content so answer engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini cite them. It includes frameworks for entity-first architecture, content audits, category creation and turning founder videos into authority systems.

Which article should I read first?

Start with “GEO vs SEO: stop ranking, start dominating answer engines”, then “Entity-first content architecture”, then “The B2B founder's complete guide to answer engine visibility”. Together they explain the shift, the architecture and the platform-level tactics.

Is GEO replacing SEO?

No. GEO builds on SEO fundamentals such as crawlability, page speed, structured data and credible backlinks. What changes is the goal: instead of chasing a ranking position, you structure content so AI models can extract and attribute specific claims to your company.

Who are these guides written for?

Founders, marketing leads and content operators at founder-led B2B companies, especially SaaS, consulting and expert-led services, who want their expertise to be visible and cited in AI-driven discovery.

Do I need technical skills to apply these frameworks?

Not to start. The audit, the terminology canon and the answer-first page structure only require editorial work. Schema markup and automation help at scale, and are the parts KORTEX usually handles for clients.

How can I check whether AI engines already cite my company?

Write down 10 to 20 questions a buyer would ask about your category, run them in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews, and record whether your company, your pages or your terminology appear. Repeat monthly with the same questions to track progress.
