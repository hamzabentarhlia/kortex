In short

ChatGPT is an excellent drafting tool, and we use language models too. What a chatbot does not do on its own is find the expertise in your content, keep it consistent across thirty formats, check every claim, add the right structure, and measure whether AI engines start citing you. If you have the time and an editor's eye, doing it yourself can work.

What ChatGPT does well

It drafts quickly, rewrites in different tones, summarises transcripts and suggests outlines. For a single post or a first draft, it is fast and cheap.

What it does not do on its own

  • Know your expertise. It writes from its training data unless you feed it your material and guide it carefully every time.
  • Check facts. It can state wrong things confidently. Someone has to verify every claim.
  • Stay consistent. Thirty assets from thirty prompts drift in vocabulary, tone and claims.
  • Structure for citation. Answer-first layout, schema markup and internal links are separate jobs.
  • Measure. It does not tell you whether AI engines now cite you.

When doing it yourself is the right call

If you publish one piece a month, have a few spare hours, and someone on your team can edit carefully, start with ChatGPT and a checklist. Our blog covers what that checklist should include.

When not to choose KORTEX

  • Your budget is under a few hundred euros a month.
  • You enjoy writing and have the time to do it well.
  • You only need occasional posts, not a system.
