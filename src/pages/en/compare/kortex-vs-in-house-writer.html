KORTEX vs hiring an in-house writer

An employee learns your company over the years. A studio arrives with a pipeline on day one. Which one you need depends on how much content you publish and how much of it comes from other people's expertise.

In short

An in-house writer gives you someone who knows the product, the people and the history, and who is available every day. KORTEX gives you a pipeline that turns the expertise you already record into linked pages, with a person checking every claim and a monthly citation check. If writing is a full-time need and your experts have little time, a hire may be the better answer.

What an in-house writer brings

Context. A good in-house writer learns your product, your customers and your tone, and can walk down the corridor to ask a question. They can react quickly to news, write the pieces that need an inside view, and own your editorial calendar. Nothing replaces that knowledge for work that depends on it.

When hiring is the better choice

  • Writing is a daily, full-time need, such as a newsroom-style publishing plan.
  • Most of your content depends on inside access that you would not share with a studio.
  • You want a writer who builds a long relationship with your customers and your product team.
  • You already have a strong editor who can direct a writer and review the work.

When not to choose KORTEX

  • You do not record calls, talks or webinars, and your experts will not give interviews. There is no source to work from.
  • You need rapid-response writing about news, several times a week.
  • You want a single person to own your voice across every channel.

Can they work together?

Yes, and it is a common setup. The in-house writer keeps the pieces that need an inside view and the editorial calendar. KORTEX turns the recordings your experts already produce into the connected pages around them. The writer reviews the output, which keeps the voice consistent.
