Key takeaways
  • A fixed list of ten buyer questions, run monthly with identical wording, tells you more than a one-off check.
  • Log the engine, the date, the answer, your page if cited, and the other sources cited. The competitor column is as useful as your own.
  • Answers vary from run to run and across features, so read trends over several months, not one result.
  • For Google, Search Console has a Generative AI performance report. It is first-party data.
  • Google warns that no third-party tool can see its internal ranking or AI systems.

Start with the question list, not the tool

The method rests on one thing: a fixed list of questions your buyers actually ask. Get them from sales calls, support tickets, the questions prospects raise in the first meeting. Write each one the way a buyer would type it, in full. Not "supplier risk software" but "which approach to supplier risk works for a mid-size manufacturer with suppliers in several countries".

Aim for ten. That is enough to see a pattern and small enough to run by hand in an afternoon. Group them by stage if you like: a few that define the problem, a few that compare approaches, a few that are close to a purchase decision.

Then freeze the wording. Changing a question between months breaks the comparison. If a question stops being relevant, retire it and add a new one with a new row, and say so in the notes.

The log

One spreadsheet, one row for each question in each engine in each month. These are the columns we use:

Columns for a citation log
ColumnWhat goes in it
DateThe day you ran the question
Engine and surfaceFor example Perplexity web, ChatGPT app, Google AI Mode. Note the surface, because they can differ
QuestionThe exact wording, copied from the list
AI answer shownYes or no. Google says AI Overviews often do not trigger, so "no answer" is a real result
You namedYes or no: does the answer mention your brand in the text
You citedYes or no: is one of your pages a linked source
Your pageThe URL cited, if any
Others citedThe other domains listed as sources
NotesAnything odd: a wrong fact about you, an outdated price, a confusion with another company

Two columns earn their place beyond the obvious. "You named" and "You cited" are different outcomes: an engine can describe you without linking you, or link you without naming you. And "Others cited" tells you who owns the answer today. Over a few months it shows you which competitor pages or third-party sites are doing the work you want to do.

The one metric to compute

Citation rate is the share of tracked questions where an engine cites your site. If you track ten questions and your page is a linked source in three, the citation rate is three out of ten for that engine that month. Keep the rate per engine, because they behave differently, and track "named" as a second rate.

That is the whole calculation. Resist the urge to add weights, indexes or a single blended score. With ten questions, a score with two decimals is false precision.

Why results move, and how to read them

Expect variation. Google's own documentation says AI Overviews are shown only when its systems determine they add to classic Search, so they often do not trigger. It also says AI Mode and AI Overviews may use different models and techniques, so the set of responses and links they show will vary. Perplexity states that its user agent fetches pages when a person asks a question. All of this means the same question can produce different sources on different days.

So read the log like this:

  • Do not react to one month. Wait for three runs before calling a change real.
  • Look for stable patterns: a question where you are cited every time, or where a competitor always is.
  • Treat a sudden drop on every question at once as a technical problem first. Check that your pages are still indexed and not blocked.
  • Record what you changed on the site and when, in the notes, so that a later change in the log can be linked to a cause.

Google's own data: Search Console

For Google, you do not have to guess. Google states that sites appearing in AI features such as AI Overviews and AI Mode are included in the overall traffic in Search Console, and its guide points to a Generative AI performance report there. Use it as the first-party record for Google, and use your manual log for the engines that do not give you one.

The two sources answer different questions. Search Console tells you how people find your pages through Google's generative features. The log tells you whether a specific buyer question gets your page as a source, across engines.

A light automation, with limits

You can automate the boring parts. In n8n, a Schedule Trigger on the first working day of the month can start a flow that reads the question list from a Google Sheets node, creates the month's empty rows, and sends an email with the Send Email node to whoever runs the check. A Code node can then calculate the citation rate from the filled rows and write it to a summary sheet.

What we do not automate is the reading of the answers. Engines return different results through different surfaces, and we do not know how closely an automated call matches what a person sees in the product. We log the surface we used, run the questions in the product people use, and read each answer. Run by hand, ten questions is a short job.

Third-party tools: what to ask

Citation tracking tools exist and some may save you time. Google's guide gives one clear warning: no third-party tool has access to its internal ranking or AI systems, and tools claiming to use internal Google metrics should be treated with care. Evaluate their advice against the official guidance.

Before you pay, ask the vendor:

  • Which prompts do you run, and can I supply my own?
  • How many times is each prompt run, and how is variation handled?
  • Which product surface do you query: the app, the website or an API?
  • Can I export the raw answers, so I can check them?

If the answers are vague, a spreadsheet is the better tool.

What to do with the results

  1. For questions where you are never cited, check whether the page exists, is indexed and answers the question directly with something specific.
  2. For questions where a competitor is cited, read their page. Ask what it contains that yours does not. Do not copy it. Add something only you have.
  3. For wrong or outdated statements about you, fix the source on your own site first and make the correct fact easy to find.
  4. For questions where you are cited, leave the page alone unless the facts change. Update the date when you do.

Common questions about measuring AI citations

How many questions should I track?

Ten is a good start. It is enough to see a pattern, small enough to run by hand each month, and easy to group by buying stage. Add more only when you can still review each answer.

How often should I run the check?

Once a month, with identical wording. Running it more often adds noise, because answers vary between runs. Judge change over at least three months.

What is the difference between being named and being cited?

Named means the answer text mentions your brand. Cited means one of your pages appears as a linked source. Either can happen without the other, so log both.

Can Search Console show AI Overview traffic?

Google says sites appearing in AI Overviews and AI Mode are included in overall Search Console traffic, and its guide points to a Generative AI performance report. It covers Google only.

Why do I see no AI answer for some questions?

Google says AI Overviews are only shown when its systems judge them additive to classic Search, so they often do not trigger. Log "no AI answer" as a result and do not count it as a miss.

Should I use an API or the product itself?

We use the product, because that is what buyers see, and we log the surface. We do not know how closely API answers match the product, so we do not assume they are the same.
