Three models buy three different things

An in-house team buys capacity that you direct every day. An agency buys a defined service delivered to a scope. An AI studio buys a repeatable process that turns something you already published into many formats. The comparison goes wrong when the three are treated as three prices for the same product, because they are three products.

A cost model with every figure labeled

We keep the in-house default on the pricing page as an editable calculator. The defaults are an assumption: €6,500 a month for an SEO lead, €5,000 for a writer and €4,200 for a social media manager, a total of €15,700. They are full monthly employer costs for experienced full-time profiles. They do not come from a salary survey and they vary a lot by country, city and seniority.

Against that, a SWARM plan is €5,500 a month for 20 to 24 assets from one source, about €230 to €275 each, with a monthly citation check. The difference on the defaults is €10,200 a month. Three caveats decide whether that difference means anything for you.

  • Hiring and management time are left out. Recruiting, software, training and the hours you spend directing the team make the real in-house figure higher.
  • The scope differs. An in-house team can handle more than one source a month and run your community. A KORTEX plan does not run your community.
  • Output is not identical. The comparison is of budgets. It assumes no commercial result and no citation, for any model.

We have no sourced figure for an agency retainer, so we do not print one. Ask for three quotes at the same scope and put them next to the other two columns.

When an in-house team is the better choice

Content is a permanent function of the company, with several sources a month, a product that changes weekly, or a community to run. An in-house writer absorbs context that an outside provider will never get from a transcript, and can walk down the corridor to ask an engineer a question. If you publish from more than two or three sources a month and you can hire well, in-house is likely the stronger base.

An outside provider only knows what you give it. If the knowledge lives in people's heads, hire the people.

When an agency is the better choice

The gap is not content. It is technical SEO, digital PR, link acquisition, paid media or a site migration. A good agency brings specialist people you would not hire full-time, and an AI studio does none of those things. Our comparison with an SEO agency sets out where the two overlap and where they do not.

The risk with any AI-assisted model

Google's spam policy lists "using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users" as an example of scaled content abuse. Its guidance on generative AI content also says it is critical to manually fact-check and review AI-generated content before publishing, including metadata and structured data. A studio that cannot show you its review step is carrying that risk for you. Ours is described in the human QA layer.

Questions to ask any provider before signing

  1. Where does the material come from, and can I see the pointer from each claim back to the source?
  2. Who checks the facts, and what do they check against?
  3. What is outside the scope? Ask for the list in writing.
  4. How do I leave, and what do I own afterwards?
  5. How will we know whether engines cite the work? A provider that promises citations is promising what only the engines decide.

For KORTEX the answers are: your own source, a person checking against it, no community management or link building, 30 days' notice with everything owned by you, and a monthly citation check that reports results as they are. The method for that check is in how to measure AI citations without a tool budget.

A rule for choosing

Ask where the missing capability is. If it is people who know your product, choose in-house. If it is a specialist skill such as links or technical work, choose an agency. If it is the packaging of expertise you already have, choose a studio. The three can also work together: an in-house lead who owns strategy, an agency for links and a studio for volume is a common arrangement, and none of the three excludes the others.

Common questions about choosing a content model

Is an AI studio cheaper than an in-house team?

On our default assumptions a SWARM plan costs €5,500 a month against €15,700 for an SEO lead, a writer and a social media manager. The scope is not identical, so read it as a comparison of budgets, with your own numbers in place of ours.

Can an AI studio replace a content team?

It can free a team from rebuilding each source in five formats. It does not replace strategy, product knowledge or community work, which stay with your people.

What does a traditional agency cost?

It varies too widely for us to give a sourced figure. Request quotes at an identical scope and compare them with the other options.

Do the models work together?

Yes. A common setup is an in-house owner of the strategy, an agency for technical work and links, and a studio for turning sources into formats.

What should I ask an AI provider about quality?

Who checks the facts, against what, and whether you can see the link from each claim back to your source. Google asks that AI-generated content is manually fact-checked before publication.

Will any of these get me cited by ChatGPT or Perplexity?

None can promise it. AI engines are third-party systems. The honest offer is better content and a regular measurement of what they do.
