Key takeaways
  • Read without scripts, the page still shows the plan prices, the calculator with its default numbers and all six FAQ answers.
  • The first price appears about 300 words into a 2,061-word page. Buyers who ask "how much does it cost" get the answer, but not at the top.
  • Nothing on the page says whether prices include VAT. That is a buyer question the page does not answer.
  • The page links to the audit and to how it works, and to no proof page. A claim of quality with nothing to check is weaker than one with a link.
  • Structured data matches the visible text: Service, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList. It is hygiene, not a ranking tactic.

Why our own page

A teardown of someone else's page needs their agreement, and we have not asked anyone. So the first one is about ours. The rule is the same for every teardown: fetch the page without scripts, count what is there, ask the questions a buyer would ask, and write down what to change.

The page is the pricing page. It matters because a buyer who has heard of KORTEX from an assistant often lands there next, and because it carries the most numbers on the site.

What we change first

  1. Add the VAT line. One sentence under the plan prices and one FAQ entry, written once the facts are confirmed. It is the cheapest change and answers a real buyer question.
  2. Link the proof. Point to the citation log from the plans section. The log shows what we measured, including where nothing changed.
  3. Move the answer up. Put the three plan prices in a short summary at the top, so the first 100 words answer the first question. The longer explanations stay where they are.

We have not made these changes yet. This page is dated, and when we do, the next teardown on this site will say what changed and what we measured afterwards.

What this does not show

A teardown reads a page. It does not tell you whether an assistant cites it. For that you ask the questions, in the buyer's words, and log what comes back: how to measure AI citations describes the method. And as Google's own documentation says, none of this markup is a requirement for its AI features, which is why we treat it as hygiene: schema markup, and what it will not do.
