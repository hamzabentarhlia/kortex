In more detail

Google's guidance on generative AI content notes that generative models do not retrieve facts but predict a likely sequence of words, which is why their outputs can contain inaccuracies. Grounding addresses that by retrieving documents first and basing the answer on them, the pattern described under retrieval-augmented generation. The sources used are the ones shown as citations.

For a publisher this has one practical consequence: a page that states specific, checkable facts in plain text gives a grounded answer something to hold on to.

An example

A buyer asks what a fair review frequency is for suppliers with a late-payment history. A grounded assistant retrieves several pages, drafts an answer from them and links the ones it used. A page that says "review monthly when payments are more than 30 days late" with its reasoning is easier to ground on than one that says "it depends".
