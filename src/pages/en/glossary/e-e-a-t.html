What Google says

Google's guidance on helpful content says its automated systems use many factors to rank content, and that they identify a mix of factors that can show experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness. Of these, trust is most important. The others contribute to trust, and content does not need to show all four. Some content is helpful because of the experience it shows, other content because of the expertise it shares.

Google also says E-E-A-T itself is not a specific ranking factor. Using a mix of factors that can identify content with good E-E-A-T is useful, and its systems give even more weight to it for topics that could significantly affect health, financial stability, safety or the well-being of society.

What it looks like on a B2B site

  • Experience: first-hand material such as your own data, cases and decisions.
  • Expertise: named authors with a real role and a track record in the subject.
  • Authoritativeness: others referring to you and your work, earned rather than bought.
  • Trust: accurate claims, cited sources, dates, clear contact details and honest limits.

What it is not

It is not a score you can read, and it is not a markup you can add. Search quality raters use these ideas to evaluate how well Google's systems work, but Google says raters have no control over how pages rank, and their data is not used directly in its ranking algorithms.

Source: Google Search Central, creating helpful, reliable, people-first content.
