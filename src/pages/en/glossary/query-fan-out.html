Google says its AI Overviews and AI Mode may use it. It means a page can be picked up for a sub-question you never saw typed. ---

How it works

When someone asks a long or complex question, the system generates several related queries and sends them together. Google's own example starts from a question about fixing a lawn full of weeds. The related searches might cover the best herbicides for lawns, removing weeds without chemicals, and preventing weeds from coming back. The results from all of them feed one response.

Why it matters for B2B content

A B2B buyer rarely asks one clean question. A single question about supplier risk can break into sub-questions on audit frequency, warning signals, cost and tools. A page that answers one of those sub-questions well has a way into the answer, even if it never targeted the original question.

The trap

Fan-out is not a reason to publish a page for every possible variation. Google says that creating separate content for each variation, mainly to manipulate rankings or AI responses, violates its scaled content abuse policy, and that it is ineffective as a long-term strategy. Write one strong page for each question that buyers really have.
