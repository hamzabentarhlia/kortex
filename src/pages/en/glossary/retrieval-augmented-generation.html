How it works and what it means for your content. h1: Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) summary: How an AI answer is built from pages fetched at the moment you ask. kind: term kicker: Glossary in_short: Retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG, is a technique where an AI system first retrieves relevant, up-to-date pages and then writes its answer from them. It is also called grounding. Its purpose is to improve the quality, accuracy and freshness of answers, and it is why AI answers can show links to the pages they relied on. ---

How it works

A language model on its own answers from what it learned in training. That knowledge is fixed at a date and can be wrong or out of date. With RAG, the system adds a step before the answer. It searches an index, retrieves the pages that look relevant, reads the specific information in them, and writes a response that is based on those pages.

Google describes its generative AI features this way. They rely on its core Search ranking systems to retrieve relevant, current pages from the Search index, and then review the information in those pages to generate a more reliable response, showing links to the pages that support it.

Why it matters for your content

If an answer is built from retrieved pages, your page has to be retrievable first. For Google, that means indexed and eligible to show a snippet. After that, what matters is whether the page contains something specific and useful to the question. A page that only restates common knowledge gives the system little reason to prefer it.

An example

A buyer asks which approach to supplier risk suits a mid-size manufacturer. The system retrieves a handful of pages on supplier risk, reads the parts that address mid-size manufacturers, and writes an answer with links. A page that names the specific situation and gives a clear recommendation has a better chance of being one of the pages read than a generic overview.

What it does not mean

RAG does not mean a special file or markup is needed. Google states that there are no extra requirements to appear in its AI features beyond being indexed and eligible for a snippet. Other engines have not published equivalent detail that we could verify.
