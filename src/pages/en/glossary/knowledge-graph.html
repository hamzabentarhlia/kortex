Search engines use graphs of this kind to understand who and what a page is about. ---

In more detail

In a knowledge graph a company is a node, its founder is another, and "founded by" is the line between them. Facts attached to nodes, such as a name, a city or a website, come from many public sources. A site owner cannot write to such a graph directly. The practical lever is to make the public information about you consistent and easy to match: one spelling of the name, a real author page, matching markup and profiles that point back to the site.

An example

Invented for illustration: a graph holds a node for a software company, a node for its founder, a node for the city where it is based and a node for its product. Pages that name the founder and the company the same way across the site and the profiles make the links between those nodes easy to confirm. Pages that use three spellings leave the match in doubt.
