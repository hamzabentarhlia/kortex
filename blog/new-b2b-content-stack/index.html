The new B2B content stack: video, entities, answers, distribution.

By Hamza Bentarhlia · Updated October 15, 2026 · 20 min read

Key takeaways
  • B2B content does not have a volume problem; it has a coherence problem. Pages that do not share one spine do not add up to authority.
  • The new stack has four layers: raw signal → entity structure → answer assets → distribution surfaces.
  • Answer engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews cite what they can clearly attribute; fragmented terminology gets synthesized around, not quoted.
  • Run one source cycle at a time, publish the answer layer before the distribution wave, and measure entity footprint, not only traffic.
  • AI tools (Whisper for transcription, Gemini for extraction and drafting, n8n for orchestration) automate the mechanics; humans still decide the thesis and approve every asset.

Why did the old B2B content stack break?

The old stack broke because it assumed discovery started with search and ended in a demo. Buyers now discover categories through AI answers, social feeds, podcasts and private communities, and a weekly blog without a stable entity footprint is too thin to be recognized as a source.

The classic B2B content machine assumed the buying process started with search, moved through blog discovery, and ended in a demo flow. That model still exists, but it is no longer the only front door. Prospects now discover categories through podcasts, social feeds, AI answers, private chats, and product-adjacent media. The old "publish a blog every week" model is too thin to cover that surface area.

The problem is not just channel fragmentation. It is message fragmentation. Teams create content across surfaces without building a stable entity footprint. The company becomes visible in bursts, but not legible at scale. An AI model encountering your content across five disconnected pages cannot reliably attribute authority to your brand. A human buyer across five disconnected posts cannot form a clear impression of what you actually do.

The B2B content crisis is not a volume problem. Teams are producing more content than ever. It is a coherence problem, content that does not add up to anything because it was never designed to share the same spine.

What did the old stack get wrong?

The old stack was built around three assumptions that no longer hold. First: that search was the primary discovery channel, so keyword density was the main optimization target. Second: that blogs were the primary content format, so publishing frequency was the main operational metric. Third: that the job of content was to generate traffic, so click-through rate was the main success indicator.

Each of those assumptions is now partially obsolete. Discovery is multi-channel and AI-mediated. Format matters less than argument density and entity clarity. And the scoreboard has shifted from traffic to citation authority, being retrievable by models is the new ranking. We unpack that shift in Stop ranking, start being cited.

What are the four layers of the new B2B content stack?

The four layers are raw signal, entity structure, answer assets and distribution surfaces. Each layer feeds the next, and every layer reuses the same definitions so the whole system reads as one coherent entity.

The new B2B content stack replaces the blog-calendar model with a source-cycle model. One high-signal input produces a coherent set of outputs across four layers, each reinforcing the same entity footprint. Here is how each layer works and what it requires.

Layer 1, Raw signal

This is the founder video, podcast episode, webinar, or expert teardown. It is where the real language lives: the precise framing, the repeated examples, the objections the founder instinctively addresses, the analogies they reach for. Raw signal is the highest-density content asset a company produces because it captures conviction, not just information.

Most teams let raw signal die in the format it was created. A webinar gets uploaded to YouTube and forgotten. A podcast episode gets a short-form clip and a generic caption. The opportunity is to treat the raw source not as a content endpoint, but as a content foundation, the material from which every other asset is extracted. The extraction method is detailed step by step in From one founder video to a 30-day authority system.

Source principle

Do not ask writers to invent authority from a blank page. Extract it from the highest-signal founder artifact you already have. The conviction is already there. The system converts it.

Layer 2, Entity structure

Entity structure is the translation step between raw signal and publishable content. It converts a founder's spoken language into a stable set of definitions that every piece of content references consistently.

The entity structure for a B2B company answers five questions with precision:

  • Category: What space do you occupy? Not "B2B SaaS" or "marketing agency," but the specific named category you are competing to define or own.
  • Problem: What specific pain does the category solve? Named precisely, not generically.
  • Mechanism: What is your model or method? The named process, framework, or system that distinguishes how you solve the problem.
  • Alternative: What do buyers do instead, and why is it insufficient? The contrast is what sharpens positioning.
  • Audience: Who is this for, stated with enough specificity that the right reader self-selects and the wrong reader self-excludes.

When these five definitions are consistent across every page, post, and asset, using the same phrases, the same named mechanisms, the same contrasts, AI models can cluster the content into a coherent entity and attribute authority to it. When the definitions vary across surfaces, the entity graph fragments and authority attribution weakens. For the full architecture, see entity-first content architecture.

Layer 3, Answer assets

Answer assets are the content pieces built specifically for retrieval. Not for traffic. Not for shares. For the moment when a prospect or a model asks a relevant question and needs a source to cite.

A complete answer asset layer for a single source cycle typically includes:

  • Pillar article: defines the core thesis with precision, the category argument, the mechanism, the use cases. This is the primary retrieval anchor.
  • Contrarian article: attacks the dominant false belief in the market. This captures readers who are already skeptical of the standard approach and looking for an alternative framing.
  • Comparison article: positions your mechanism against the standard alternative. This captures mid-funnel demand from buyers who are already evaluating options.
  • FAQ cluster: maps the follow-up questions your market asks after encountering the core thesis. Question-and-answer structures give AI models clean, self-contained passages they can extract and attribute.
  • Use-case blocks: short-form descriptions of how the mechanism applies to specific buyer contexts. These drive conversion from readers who self-identify with a described scenario.

What makes an asset an "answer asset" is its shape: a question-shaped heading, a direct answer in the first one or two sentences, then depth, definitions, steps, comparisons, objections. Our answer engine visibility guide covers the formatting rules in detail.

Layer 4, Distribution surfaces

LinkedIn, email, short-form clips, community drops, and outreach sequences all reinforce the same entity structure. Distribution is no longer a separate function from content. It is the repeated exposure layer of the same thesis, the mechanism by which the entity graph accumulates surface area.

The key operational principle is that distribution surfaces should be derivative of the answer asset layer, not independent of it. A LinkedIn post should point to an article. An email newsletter should reference the same FAQ cluster that the article links to. An outreach sequence should use the same mechanism language that the pillar article establishes. When every surface speaks the same language and routes to the same source layer, the authority compounds. Posts that ignore this rule are the ones that die in 48 hours.

Which AI tools does the new content stack need?

The stack needs four tool categories: speech-to-text to turn the source into a transcript, a large language model to extract and draft, an orchestration layer to chain the steps, and your existing CMS and channels to publish. At KORTEX this is Whisper, Gemini and n8n, with human checkpoints at the decisions that matter.

Tools are the least strategic part of the stack, which is precisely why they should be chosen last. A workflow that automates a fragmented process only produces fragmentation faster. Once the four layers are defined, though, automation removes most of the repetitive work between them.

What does an n8n content automation workflow look like?

n8n is a workflow automation tool that chains steps, triggers, API calls, conditions, human approvals, into one repeatable pipeline. A typical content workflow built on it runs like this:

  1. Trigger: a new source link is submitted through a form, or a recording lands in a shared folder.
  2. Transcription: the audio is sent to Whisper and comes back as a timestamped transcript.
  3. Extraction: Gemini tags the six extraction elements (thesis, mechanism, rejected alternative, repeated examples, audience signals, objections) and returns them as structured data.
  4. Human checkpoint #1: an editor turns that output into the entity sheet and the founder validates the thesis and mechanism name. Nothing downstream runs before this approval.
  5. Drafting: each answer asset is drafted from its own template, with the entity sheet and glossary injected into every prompt so terminology cannot drift.
  6. Automated checks: forbidden synonyms, missing internal links, an opening paragraph that does not answer the heading's question.
  7. Human checkpoint #2: editing and approval, then scheduling according to the publishing sequence.

The two human checkpoints are not optional. They are where judgment lives, and where the difference between a cited source and generic AI content is decided.

What are the common mistakes with AI content tools?

  • Prompting from a blank page. Asking a model to "write a post about X" produces the average of the internet. Always prompt from the source and the entity sheet.
  • Letting each prompt invent vocabulary. Without an injected glossary, every draft rephrases your mechanism differently, the fastest way to fragment an entity.
  • Automating publication without review. Unreviewed claims are a reputational risk, and answer engines do not need more unverified content.
  • Buying tools before defining layers. The workflow mirrors the model; without the model, there is nothing to automate.

Why do coherent systems outperform fragmented channels?

Coherent systems win because they create one recognizable entity footprint that both human buyers and AI models can cluster, remember and retrieve. The new stack often produces less content than a blog calendar, but every piece reinforces the others.

The reason the four-layer model outperforms the blog-calendar model is not that it produces more content. It often produces less. The reason is coherence.

For human buyers

Buyers who encounter your content across multiple surfaces, a LinkedIn post, then an article, then a comparison page, then an email, form a stronger and faster impression of what you do when those surfaces share the same language and thesis. The message accumulates. The category association strengthens. The path to consideration shortens.

When those surfaces use different language, reference different frameworks, and point to different ideas, the buyer experience is fragmented. They might engage with each piece individually, but they do not form a coherent impression of your company as a source of authority on a specific problem.

For AI answer engines

Answer engines assemble responses from sources they can confidently associate with a topic. When your company uses the same category terms, the same mechanism names, and the same contrasting alternatives across every page and post, those signals reinforce one another and point to one clear entity, which makes your pages easier to retrieve and to cite when a relevant question arrives.

When your content is fragmented, different terminology per page, different positioning per post, no internal linking to reinforce the same thesis, there is no strong, unambiguous entity to point to. Your ideas might still shape an answer, but your company will not be cited as the authority. It will be synthesized around, not quoted. This is the core of Generative Engine Optimization (GEO).

The difference between being cited and being synthesized around is entity clarity. A model cites what it can clearly attribute. It synthesizes what is too fragmented to name.

What happens after publication? Distribution is the midpoint, not the finish line

Once an answer asset is live, you slice it into three demand states, top-of-funnel opinions, mid-funnel comparisons and bottom-of-funnel action blocks, and connect them with internal links so readers and models can follow the path.

Publishing the article is the midpoint, not the finish line. The same thesis travels across all three demand states, but the framing changes depending on where the reader sits in the decision.

Top-of-funnel distribution

At the top of the funnel, buyers are not yet aware they have a problem you solve. Content here should name and frame the problem in a way that creates recognition, the "that is exactly what is happening to us" response. LinkedIn posts, podcast clips, and newsletter introductions are the primary surfaces. The goal is not to sell the solution. The goal is to create category awareness and pull the reader toward the deeper asset.

Mid-funnel distribution

At the mid-funnel, buyers know the problem exists and are evaluating approaches. Content here should sharpen the contrast between your mechanism and the alternative, the comparison article, the contrarian take, the decision framework. LinkedIn posts that pose "when to do X vs Y" questions, emails that break down the tradeoffs, and solution-page copy that addresses objections directly all serve this stage. The goal is to become the preferred framing for a buyer who is already in evaluation mode.

Bottom-of-funnel distribution

At the bottom of the funnel, buyers are ready to act but need a specific reason. Content here should be concrete: process breakdowns, use-case descriptions, CTA blocks tied to a specific next step. The FAQ cluster serves this stage strongly, buyers in late evaluation ask very specific questions, and a well-built FAQ cluster can answer them before the buyer has to reach out.

Internal linking as a distribution layer

Internal linking is a distribution mechanism, not just an SEO tactic. A pricing page that reinforces the solution page. A solution page that reinforces the pillar article. The article that links to the FAQ cluster and the contact page. This architecture creates a coherent path from awareness to conversion that both search engines and AI models can follow, and that human buyers can follow without friction.

How should teams actually execute the new stack?

Run one complete source cycle at a time, sequence the outputs so the answer layer is live before the distribution wave, and track entity signals alongside traffic.

The strategic model is clear. The execution challenge is operational. Most B2B teams default to fragmented content production because it requires less upfront design. The new stack requires more planning but less ongoing effort, because every new source cycle adds to an existing coherent system rather than starting from a blank page.

Start with one source cycle, not a content calendar

Reduce content chaos by running one source cycle at a time. Pick one long-form source. Extract the category thesis. Build the entity structure from it. Produce the answer asset layer. Distribute from there. Do not start a second source cycle until the first one is fully deployed across all layers.

The discipline is counterintuitive for teams used to publishing volume. But the more fragmented your inputs, the weaker your entity graph. A company with three complete, coherent source cycles has a clearer authority footprint than a company with 50 disconnected blog posts.

Sequence the outputs, not just the topics

Sequencing matters because different assets serve different stages of the buying process and the retrieval cycle. The pillar article should be live before the LinkedIn posts that reference it. The FAQ cluster should be live before the outreach sequences that answer common objections. The comparison page should be live before the email that positions against the alternative.

The sequence that works in practice: pillar article first, then contrarian article, then comparison page, then FAQ cluster, then LinkedIn distribution wave, then email amplification. Each asset that goes live strengthens the retrieval potential of the ones that preceded it.

Measure entity footprint, not just traffic

The traditional content metric, organic traffic, is insufficient for the new stack. You also need to track entity signals. A simple monthly routine:

  • Prompt panel: keep a fixed list of 15–25 category questions your buyers ask, run them in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews each month, and log whether your pages are cited or your company is named.
  • Buyer language: note in your CRM when inbound leads use your mechanism name or category label unprompted.
  • Source pages: check which of your pages are linked to or referenced by adjacent publications.
  • Terminology audit: once per cycle, search your own site and posts for forbidden synonyms that crept back in. Our content audit for AI retrieval gives a full method.

These signals indicate whether the authority system is working, independent of whether the traffic numbers are moving.

  • Answer assets: pillar "What is manager-led onboarding?", contrarian "Why onboarding portals do not onboard anyone", comparison "Manager-led vs HR-led onboarding", FAQ of 12 questions, three use-case blocks (engineering, sales, customer success).

Every asset uses the same five definitions. That is the whole trick, and the whole discipline.

Who is the new content stack built for?

It is built for founder-led B2B companies, expert-led consultancies and operator-led SaaS teams whose best thinking already exists in raw form, videos, podcasts, webinars, sales calls.

The four-layer model is specifically suited to companies where the highest-quality content signal already exists inside the founder's head, and in their existing video, podcast, and webinar archive.

It is not suited to companies that have nothing to say. The model extracts and amplifies real conviction. It cannot manufacture conviction that does not exist. The prerequisite is not a polished content strategy. It is a founder or operator with a genuine point of view on a real problem, expressed somewhere in raw form.

If that source material exists, and in most founder-led companies it does, buried in YouTube, Loom, podcast backlogs, and sales call recordings, the stack can convert it into a compounding authority system that generates retrievable, citable, commercial content from a single input cycle.

Frequently asked questions about the new B2B content stack

What is the new B2B content stack?

The new B2B content stack is a four-layer model built for AI-native discovery: raw signal (a founder video, podcast or webinar), entity structure (stable definitions of category, problem, mechanism, alternative and audience), answer assets (pillar articles, comparison pages, FAQ clusters and use-case blocks built to be retrieved and cited) and distribution surfaces (LinkedIn, email and community channels that repeatedly expose the same thesis).

Why is the old B2B content model broken?

The old model assumed the buying process started with search and ended in a demo. Prospects now discover categories through AI answers, social feeds, podcasts and private communities, not just search. Teams publishing weekly blogs without a stable entity footprint become visible in bursts but are not legible as a source at scale, so answer engines have no clear entity to cite.

What is entity structure in B2B content?

Entity structure is the stable set of definitions a company uses across its content: the category it belongs to, the problem it solves, the mechanism it uses, the alternative it rejects and the audience it serves. When these definitions are identical on every page, post and asset, AI models can associate the content with the company reliably and cite it.

How is GEO different from SEO in a B2B content context?

SEO optimizes content to rank on search result pages. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) optimizes content to be cited by answer engines such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews. GEO prioritizes entity clarity, answer-first formatting, a clear point of view and internal source reinforcement, whereas SEO has historically focused on keywords, backlinks and click-through rates. The two are complementary, not opposed.

Which AI tools do you need to automate content with n8n?

A minimal stack has three components: a speech-to-text model such as Whisper to transcribe the source, a large language model such as Gemini to extract the thesis and draft assets, and n8n to orchestrate the steps, inject the entity sheet into every prompt and route drafts to human approval. Your existing CMS and publishing tools handle the last mile. The tools matter less than the two human checkpoints: entity validation and final editing.

How is this different from content repurposing?

Content repurposing converts one format into another without changing the strategic architecture: a blog post becomes a carousel, a video becomes a transcript. The new stack relies on source extraction instead: every output is built to serve a specific stage of the buying process and of the retrieval cycle, with the same entity structure running through all of them. Repurposing produces formats; source extraction produces a coherent authority system.

How many content pieces should come from one source cycle?

One strong source cycle, built from a single founder video or podcast, should produce one pillar article, one contrarian article, one comparison piece, one FAQ cluster, 8–12 LinkedIn posts, 2 newsletter angles and supporting sales assets such as CTA blocks and call scripts. The same narrative spine runs through every output.

How long does it take to see results from the new stack?

There is no guaranteed timeline, because answer engines do not publish how quickly they pick up new sources. As an indicative order of magnitude: LinkedIn engagement signals can appear within the first weeks of distribution; appearances in AI answers usually require the answer layer to be live and indexed for several weeks; durable entity authority builds over several months of consistent source cycles. Plan in quarters, not weeks.

What if we have no existing video content to use as a source?

Video is the ideal raw signal, but the source can also be a founder interview, a series of sales call recordings, an existing thought-leadership document or a conversation recorded for this purpose. If no raw source exists, creating one is the first step: a 45–60 minute recorded conversation with the founder, structured around the company's core thesis, is enough to launch the first source cycle.

How do we know if our entity structure is working?

The clearest signal is linguistic: buyers who contact you use the same terminology your content uses, such as your mechanism name, your category label or your contrasting alternative. Other signals include answer engines citing your pages for category questions in a monthly prompt panel, other creators referencing your frameworks, and inbound leads who can articulate your positioning before the first conversation.
